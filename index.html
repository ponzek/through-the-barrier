3

Relative probability density

How does the magnitude of the quantum state vary across the barrier?

Hover to read actual values. Left of the barrier, incident and reflected waves interfere, so |ψ|² ripples between (1−√R)² and (1+√R)². Right of the barrier it is flat at T.

Scattering states are not square-normalizable, so |ψ|² is a relative density (incident amplitude = 1), not an absolute position probability.

4

Reflection versus transmission

How much of the incident probability current is reflected, and how much transmitted?

R and T are computed two ways: from the closed-form formula and from the solved wavefunction coefficients (|B|², |F|²). Their agreement and R + T = 1 are shown live.

Tunneling does not cost energy. A transmitted particle leaves with the same E it arrived with; only the probability of transmission is reduced.

Click the map to move the marker (sets V₀ and a)

Does the implementation reproduce known physics?

Model, variables, and assumptions

Time-independent Schrödinger equation for an electron (m = 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg) on a rectangular barrier V(x) = V₀ for 0 ≤ x ≤ a, else 0. κ = √(2m(V₀−E))/ħ;  T = [1 + V₀² sinh²(κa) / (4E(V₀−E))]⁻¹ for E < V₀;  T = [1 + V₀² sin²(qa) / (4E(E−V₀))]⁻¹ for E > V₀;  T = [1 + mV₀a²/(2ħ²)]⁻¹ at E = V₀;  R + T = 1.
